## The Intake Protocol

> **When**: First time uploading papers  
> **Purpose**: Map the landscape before diving deep

**Prompt:**
> I'm going to share [X] papers on [topic]. Before I ask anything, do this:
> 1. List every paper by author + year + core claim in one sentence
> 2. Group them into clusters of shared assumptions
> 3. Flag any paper that contradicts another
> 
> Don't summarize. Map the landscape.

**Why it works**: Establishes baseline understanding and identifies faultlines in the literature before you get invested in any single interpretation.

#landscape-mapping #baseline

## The Contradiction Finder

> **When**: Early analysis phase  
> **Purpose**: Surface disagreements that reveal hidden assumptions

**Prompt:**
> Across all papers uploaded, identify every point where two or more authors directly contradict each other. For each contradiction:
> - State both positions
> - Name the papers
> - Explain WHY they likely disagree (methodology, dataset, era)
> 
> Format as a table.

**Why it works**: Most researchers miss contradictions entirely. This prompt forces you to see where the field genuinely disagrees—and why. These gaps are where breakthroughs happen.

#contradictions #critical-analysis #debate-mapping

## The Citation Chain

> **When**: Understanding intellectual lineage  
> **Purpose**: Trace concepts through time, not just across space

**Prompt:**
> Pick the 3 most-cited concepts across these papers. For each concept:
> - Who introduced it first?
> - Who challenged it?
> - Who refined it?
> - What's the current consensus (if any)?
> 
> Show me the intellectual lineage like a family tree.

**Why it works**: Saves ~6 hours of backward citation digging. You see which ideas evolved and which stayed stuck, revealing where the field is stale vs. dynamic.

#intellectual-history #concept-evolution #citation-analysis

## The Methodology Audit

> **When**: Understanding research rigor  
> **Purpose**: Identify methodological patterns and gaps

**Prompt:**
> Compare the research methodologies used across all papers. Group by:
> - Surveys
> - Experiments
> - Simulations
> - Meta-analyses
> - Case studies
> 
> Then flag:
> - Which methodology dominates this field and why?
> - Which methodology is underused?
> - Which paper's methodology is weakest and why?

**Why it works**: Reveals whether a field is over-reliant on one approach and thus vulnerable to systematic bias.

#methodology #research-design #methodological-gaps

## The Gap Scanner

> **When**: Identifying research opportunities  
> **Purpose**: Find the white space nobody has tackled

**Prompt:**
> Based on all uploaded papers, identify the 5 research questions that NOBODY has fully answered yet. For each gap:
> - Why does it exist? (too hard, too niche, overlooked?)
> - Which existing paper came closest to answering it?
> - What methodology would be needed to close it?

**Outcome**: A prioritized list of research directions. This is where future papers should go.

> [!TIP] This is where it gets scary good—you find the questions the field has collectively ignored.

#research-gaps #frontier-questions #opportunity-analysis

## The Knowledge Map Builder

> **When**: Preparing to present or teach  
> **Purpose**: Create a visual, structural overview

**Prompt:**
> Create a structured knowledge map of this entire literature. Format:
> - **Central claim** the field orbits around
> - **3-5 supporting pillars** (well-established sub-claims)
> - **2-3 contested zones** (active debates)
> - **1-2 frontier questions** (nobody's solved yet)
> - **3 papers a newcomer MUST read first** and why
> 
> Output as a clean outline, not prose.

**Outcome**: Print this. Pin it above your desk. Refer to it constantly.

> [!NOTE] This becomes your reference architecture for the entire field—perfect for onboarding others or structuring a literature review.

#knowledge-architecture #visual-mapping #field-overview

## The Master Synthesis

> **When**: Ready to draw conclusions  
> **Purpose**: Extract collective truth from the literature

**Prompt:**
> You now have a full picture of this literature. Write a synthesis that does NOT summarize individual papers. Instead:
> - State what the field collectively believes
> - State what remains contested
> - State what's been proven beyond reasonable doubt
> - End with the single most important unanswered question
> 
> Max 400 words. No filler.

**Why it works**: Synthesis ≠ summary. This forces you to identify what's actually true vs. what's just commonly repeated.

#synthesis #consensus-building #truth-extraction

## The Assumption Killer

> **When**: Preparing original research  
> **Purpose**: Identify the unstated foundations of a field

**Prompt:**
> List every assumption that the MAJORITY of these papers share but never explicitly test or justify. For each assumption:
> - State it clearly
> - Name 1-2 papers that rely on it most
> - Explain what would happen to the field if the assumption turned out to be wrong

**Why it works**: This is how paradigm-shifting papers get written. You find the unexamined axioms everyone accepts.

> [!CAUTION] **High impact, high risk**: Challenging core assumptions is how you get ignored—or how you change everything.

#assumptions #paradigm-shift #foundational-critique

## The "So What" Test

> **When**: Final reality check  
> **Purpose**: Strip jargon and extract real-world meaning

**Prompt:**
> Pretend I have to explain this entire body of research to a smart non-expert in 5 minutes. Give me:
> 1. The one-sentence version of what this field has proven
> 2. The one honest admission of what it still doesn't know
> 3. The single real-world implication that matters most
> 
> No jargon. No hedging. No academic throat-clearing.

**Why it works**: Forces you to separate signal from noise. If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it.

> [!SUCCESS] Run this last. Every time. It's your quality check.

#clarity #real-world-impact #simplification

## Workflow: Optimal Sequencing

**Recommended order for maximum insight:**

```
1. Intake Protocol        → Establish landscape
2. Contradiction Finder   → Surface disagreements
3. Citation Chain         → Trace intellectual history
4. Methodology Audit      → Check methodological rigor
5. Gap Scanner            → Identify opportunities
6. Knowledge Map Builder  → Create structural overview
7. Assumption Killer      → Find the paradigm shifts
8. Master Synthesis       → Draw conclusions
9. So What Test           → Extract real-world meaning
```

> [!NOTE] You don't have to use all 9 every time. For quick papers: run #1, #8, #9. For deep dives: use all 9 in order.

## Key Principles

> **Synthesis ≠ Summary**
> You're not rehashing papers—you're extracting what's actually true across them.

> **Contradictions are features, not bugs**
> Where researchers disagree, the field is still learning.

> **Assumptions are the frontier**
> The unexamined axioms in a field are where breakthroughs live.
