## Structural & Clarity Prompts

### The 5 Essential Questions

> **Purpose**: Force structure instead of generic summaries  
> **When**: Starting analysis or organizing thoughts

**Prompt:**
> Analyze all inputs and generate 5 essential questions that, when answered, capture the main points and core meaning of all inputs.
>
> When formulating questions:
> - Address the central theme or argument
> - Identify key supporting ideas
> - Highlight important facts or evidence
> - Reveal the author's purpose or perspective
> - Explore significant implications or conclusions
>
> Answer all generated questions one by one in detail.

**Why it works:** This went viral for a reason. Forces you to think structurally instead of defaulting to weak summaries.

> [!TIP] Use this first. It shapes everything else.

#structure #essential-thinking #clarity

---

### Quote-First Summary

> **Purpose**: Ground every point in actual text, no paraphrasing mush  
> **When**: After initial analysis, before synthesis

**Prompt:**
> What are the 5 most important claims in these sources? For each claim:
> - Quote the exact sentence
> - Cite the specific source
> - Explain why this claim matters
> - Show how it connects to other claims
>
> Do not paraphrase. Use direct quotes only.

**Why it works:** Forces fidelity to source material. Paraphrasing is where meaning dies.

> [!NOTE] Direct quotes are your anchor. Everything else is inference.

#source-grounding #evidence-based #accuracy

---

### What's Missing?

> **Purpose**: Smart analysis focuses on omissions, not just what's there  
> **When**: During deep review phase

**Prompt:**
> Analyze all uploaded materials and identify what is NOT covered. Specifically:
> - Critical data or perspectives that should exist but don't
> - Assumptions made without strong evidence
> - Contradictions between sources (quote both sides)
> - 5 follow-up research questions to close these gaps
>
> Avoid summaries. Focus on omissions and weaknesses.

**Why it works:** Most people summarize what's there. Smart people look for what's missing. Gaps reveal where you need to dig deeper.

#gaps #critical-analysis #completeness

## Analysis & Depth Prompts

### Find The Interesting Bits

> **Purpose**: Extract surprising, non-obvious insights  
> **When**: Early in your reading/research

**Prompt:**
> From all uploaded sources, extract the most surprising, unexpected, or especially interesting insights. For each insight:
> - Explain why it's surprising
> - Include a direct quote from the source
> - Focus specifically on non-obvious patterns
>
> Avoid generic or obvious points.

**Real example:** Steven Johnson (NotebookLM director) used this on 500,000 words of NASA transcripts. Did 10 hours of work in 20 seconds.

> [!SUCCESS] This is how you escape the obviousness trap.

#insights #pattern-recognition #non-obvious

---

### The Contradiction Finder

> **Purpose**: Surface disagreements that explain why conclusions diverge  
> **When**: Comparing multiple sources or frameworks

**Prompt:**
> Identify major contradictions or conflicting findings across my sources. For each contradiction:
> - Quote both opposing claims with citations
> - Explain possible reasons for disagreement (method, timeframe, assumptions)
> - Describe what evidence would resolve the conflict
>
> Ignore minor differences. Focus on conflicts that change conclusions.

**Why it works:** Finds disagreements you'd miss reading manually. Catches methodological differences that explain divergence.

#contradictions #disagreement #critical-gaps

---

### Hidden Connections

> **Purpose**: Find non-obvious bridges between concepts  
> **When**: Synthesizing across multiple sources

**Prompt:**
> What connections exist between different concepts in my sources that aren't stated explicitly? For each connection:
> - Show relevant quotes from each source
> - Explain the implied relationship
> - Identify potential applications of this connection
>
> Avoid surface-level links. Find non-obvious bridges between concepts.

**Why it works:** Innovation lives between ideas, not inside them.

> [!TIP] The best insights hide in the intersections.

#synthesis #creativity #connections

## Critical Thinking Prompts

### The Dialectical Lens

> **Purpose**: Think with the material, not just memorize it  
> **When**: Deepening understanding of a concept

**Prompt:**
> Construct a debate between two imaginary scholars who interpret this concept in opposing ways. What evidence from the text would each one use to support their view? How would each scholar counter the other's strongest argument? Which interpretation is more defensible based on the sources?

**Why it works:** Stops rote learning. Forces active, critical engagement.

#debate #critical-thinking #perspective-taking

---

### The Anti-Thesis Method

> **Purpose**: Red-team your own thinking  
> **When**: Before finalizing any major argument

**Prompt:**
> Take the central thesis in this material and explore its opposite. What would the author have to prove if defending the reverse argument? Are there any hints in the text that unintentionally support the opposite view? What's the strongest evidence against the main argument? How would a skeptic challenge this thesis?

**Why it works:** Forces you to see where your argument is weakest.

> [!CAUTION] This is uncomfortable. That's why it works.

#counter-argument #weakness-finding #intellectual-humility

---

### The Spider Web Perspective

> **Purpose**: Map all interconnected ideas around a core concept  
> **When**: System-level understanding needed

**Prompt:**
> Map out all interconnected ideas around this core concept. What other topics, assumptions, or implications does it:
> - Silently depend on?
> - Challenge or contradict?
> - Enable or unlock?
> - Require for validity?
>
> Create a web of relationships. Show how removing one idea affects the others.

**Why it works:** Reveals fragility and strength in your argument's architecture.

#systems-thinking #interconnection #dependencies

## Rigor & Evidence Prompts

### Evidence-to-Claim Ratio Analyzer

> **Purpose**: Catch overclaiming before readers do  
> **When**: In editing phase

**Prompt:**
> Count claims vs. supporting evidence. For each section:
> - List all claims made
> - List all evidence provided
> - Calculate the ratio
> - Flag claims with weak or missing support
>
> Rank sections by evidence strength. Identify the 3 sections where I'm overclaiming most.

**Why it works:** Most writers claim too much and support too little. This catches it cold.

#evidence #rigor #claims

---

### Implementation Bridge

> **Purpose**: Turn theory into action  
> **When**: Making work applicable to real contexts

**Prompt:**
> Based on these sources, create an actionable implementation plan. Include:
> - Specific steps to apply these findings
> - Timeline for each phase
> - Required resources
> - Potential obstacles with solutions
> - Success metrics
>
> Every step must cite a specific source passage.

**Why it works:** Theory divorced from action is just essay-writing. This forces practicality.

#application #actionable #implementation

---

### The Future Scholar Perspective

> **Purpose**: See your work through critical, future eyes  
> **When**: Final review before publishing

**Prompt:**
> A hundred years from now, a scholar is analyzing this work. What would they criticize or find outdated? What would they find revolutionary or prescient? What cultural blind spots would be obvious to them? What questions would they wish the author had addressed?
>
> Be specific and cite passages.

**Why it works:** Makes you write for durability, not just for now.

> [!NOTE] This test separates timeless from trendy.

#future-proofing #timelessness #blind-spots

## Polish & Style Prompts

### Voice Consistency Audit

> **Purpose**: Catch tone drift that kills writing quality  
> **When**: During editing phase

**Prompt:**
> Analyze my tone and voice throughout the piece. Note:
> - Formal vs. casual shifts
> - Emotional tone changes (objective to passionate)
> - Person shifts (I/you/we inconsistency)
> - Register mismatches
> - Passive vs. active voice patterns
>
> Highlight 5 spots where tone breaks. Explain why and suggest consistent alternatives.

**Why it works:** Style drifts kill your credibility. This catches what you miss in self-editing.

#voice #consistency #style

---

### The Cultural Mirror

> **Purpose**: Test if ideas hold across different contexts  
> **When**: Checking universality of claims

**Prompt:**
> How would this idea look in a completely different cultural, historical, or philosophical context? Rewrite the argument from the viewpoint of:
> - A Stoic philosopher
> - A modern skeptic
> - Someone from a collectivist culture
>
> Would the core claim still hold? What would need to change?

**Why it works:** Reveals parochialism and cultural assumptions hiding in your argument.

> [!TIP] If your idea only works in one context, it's not as strong as you think.

#universality #cultural-awareness #perspective

---

### Final Polish Pass

> **Purpose**: Professional editing before publishing  
> **When**: Absolutely last step, right before hitting publish

**Prompt:**
> Run a final editing pass. Catch:
> - Overused words (use frequency analysis)
> - Clichés and tired phrases
> - Hedging language when I should be direct
> - Inconsistent formatting or style
> - Anything that sounds like AI wrote it
> - Vague claims needing concrete examples
>
> Provide a clean edit with specific fixes for each issue.

**Why it works:** Your last defense against looking sloppy or AI-written.

> [!SUCCESS] Run before publishing anything.

#editing #polish #final-check

## Optimal Workflow

### Recommended Sequencing

```
Phase 1: Understanding (30 min)
├─ The 5 Essential Questions
├─ Find The Interesting Bits
└─ Quote-First Summary

Phase 2: Analysis (1-2 hours)
├─ The Contradiction Finder
├─ Hidden Connections
├─ What's Missing?
└─ The Spider Web Perspective

Phase 3: Critical Thinking (1-2 hours)
├─ The Dialectical Lens
├─ The Anti-Thesis Method
└─ The Future Scholar Perspective

Phase 4: Rigor & Application (1-2 hours)
├─ Evidence-to-Claim Ratio Analyzer
├─ Implementation Bridge
└─ The Cultural Mirror

Phase 5: Polish (30 min)
├─ Voice Consistency Audit
└─ Final Polish Pass
```

> [!NOTE] For quick work: use Phases 1 + 5. For publication: run all phases.
